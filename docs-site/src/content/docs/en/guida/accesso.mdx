You sign in to SiteAssistant with your **email** and **password**, or with a **passkey** if you have registered one. There is no public registration: the first step is always an invitation from whoever administers the application.

## First sign-in: three steps

What it is for: signing in for the first time from your invitation, with no prior knowledge of the application.

1. **Open the invitation link.** Find the email **Your SiteAssistant invitation** and open the **Activate your account** link within **72 hours**, from your phone or your computer. The link works once.
2. **Choose your password.** On the **Choose your password** page type it twice: from **12 to 1024 characters**, your choice. No password ever arrives by email.
3. **Enter the site.** Press **Activate account**: the account is created and the session opens on its own. Choose your site from the menu at the top.

If you see **No site access**, your account is still active: ask the site owner to assign you a role. Did you receive no invitation? Go to [If you do not have an account yet](#if-you-do-not-have-an-account-yet).

## If you received an invitation

- **The link lasts 72 hours and can be used once.**
- **You choose the password**, from 12 to 1024 characters, confirming it twice on the activation page.
- **No password by email.** The invitation email contains only the link: the person inviting you never sees your password and will never ask for it by email.
- **If the link does not work** because it expired, was used or was revoked, the page answers **Ask your administrator for a new invitation**: have a new invitation sent and repeat the three steps.
- **The invitation language** sets the language of the email, of the activation page and of the new account; you can change it later in [Settings](../impostazioni/).
- The activation page requires **JavaScript** in the browser. After too many close-together attempts it asks you to try again later.

## Your passkeys

A passkey replaces the password with a check you do on your device — face, fingerprint, PIN or security key. The biometric data never leaves the device: the application only receives the confirmation that the passkey is yours.

### Creating a passkey

1. Sign in to the application and open the **gear** at the top right.
2. In the **Your account** section press **Security**: you find your passkeys, each with its **Name**, creation date and last use, or **Never used**.
3. Press **Add passkey** and confirm the window the browser opens. If the passkey is already registered on the account, the application says so.

What your device asks for depends on the operating system. Here is what the official documentation of the three platform vendors says:

- **Windows (Windows Hello).** When the browser asks where to save the passkey, you can choose to keep it on the device: Windows stores it locally with Windows Hello and asks for your usual unlock — face, fingerprint or PIN. To have it on other devices too, save it in a syncing password manager such as Microsoft Password Manager, or use a security key. Source: [Create and save a passkey — Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/accounts-billing/security/create-save-passkey).
- **Mac, iPhone and iPad (Face ID / Touch ID).** On Apple devices a passkey is authorised with Face ID or Touch ID and stored in iCloud Keychain, which syncs it across the devices of the same Apple Account; it also works from nearby non-Apple devices. Source: [About the security of passkeys — Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102195).
- **Android (Google Password Manager).** Check the details shown and confirm with the **device screen lock**: fingerprint, face, PIN or pattern. The passkey is saved and synced by Google Password Manager — or by another manager you select, such as Samsung Pass, Keeper or 1Password — and you can use it on the other Android devices that sync with it. Source: [Sign-in to your applications & websites with passkeys — Android Help](https://support.google.com/android/answer/14124480).

The confirmation window is drawn by the browser and the operating system, not by SiteAssistant: if your screen shows different words, follow the official platform guide cited above.

### Signing in with a passkey

What it is for: opening a session without typing your password.

Below the **Sign in** button the form offers two commands:

1. **Sign in with a passkey** uses the email written in the **Email** field and asks the browser for that account's passkey. The email must be filled in: if the field is empty the browser flags it and no passkey is requested.
2. **Use a passkey without email** asks for nothing: the browser offers the passkeys saved for this site and you choose one.

In both cases the confirmation happens in the browser — fingerprint, face, PIN or security key — and none of that data leaves the browser.

If the browser does not support passkeys, or the page is not open over a secure connection, the two buttons stay disabled and a note asks you to use your password. If passkeys are not configured on the server, the message says so. If you cancel the browser window or let it expire, the operation stops without opening a session: you can try again or use your password.

### Renaming or deleting a passkey

From the **Security** window:

- **Rename** changes the passkey's **Name**, from 1 to 100 characters: it helps tell apart, for example, the phone from the laptop.
- **Delete** removes the passkey after a confirmation, and with it the sessions opened with that passkey.

The last remaining passkey cannot be deleted: without it you could no longer sign back in without a password. The message says so and the list stays as it was. If you have no passkeys yet the window says so and reminds you that you can keep using your password.

A passkey belongs to your account, but its private copy stays on the device or in the manager that saved it: with iCloud Keychain you find it again on your Apple devices, with Google Password Manager on the Android devices that sync with it, while a passkey saved in Windows Hello stays on that device. See [Settings](../impostazioni/) for the other preferences.

## Sign in

What it is for: opening a session with your email and password.

1. Open the application address in your browser, on desktop or mobile.
2. If there is no active session, the **Sign in** form appears.
3. Enter your **email** and **password**, then press **Sign in**. The **◉** button next to the password field shows the password you typed; press it again to hide it.
4. After signing in you only see the sites you belong to, and you can choose your working context from the menu at the top.

The email is case-insensitive: `Mario.Rossi@example.com` and `mario.rossi@example.com` are the same account.

At the top of the demo sign-in screen, a note says which sites hold Italian data and which hold English data. After sign-in, the same note appears at the top of the **Overview**. You can dismiss the separate **Demo data** notice. See [Demo data](../dati-dimostrativi/#demo-notice-and-limits).

:::caution[If sign-in fails]
- The error message never reveals whether the email or the password is wrong.
- After **10 failed password sign-ins** in **15 minutes** for the same email and network address, the next attempt is blocked. The form shows a notice with the wait in minutes and disables **Sign in** while the button counts down. The button becomes available again when the countdown ends.
- A successful password sign-in clears the failed attempts for that email and network address. While you wait, you can use a registered passkey if one is available.
:::

## If you do not have an account yet

What it is for: getting an account when nobody has invited you.

From the sign-in form you can press **Request access**:

1. Enter your email.
2. The request reaches whoever administers the application, who can **approve** or **reject** it.
3. If approved, you receive a **temporary password** by email.
4. On first sign-in the application asks you to **change the password**. The **Change password** button is also in **Settings**, under **Your account**.

A request does not create the account on its own: it needs an administrator's approval. The review link the administrator receives expires after **72 hours** and can be used once: if it has expired, the request must be sent again.

:::caution[If the request does not arrive]
The request stays pending: it does not create the account until an administrator approves it. If the approval email does not arrive, ask the administrator to check the request and send it again only if it has expired.
:::

## Forgotten or temporary password

- **Forgot password.** On the sign-in page, select **Forgot password** and enter your email. If the account exists, you receive a single-use link that expires in **30 minutes**. Your current password stays valid until you confirm a new one. Confirming signs out every session.
- **Change password.** After signing in with a temporary password, select **Change password** to unlock the site. You need a connection and a new password that differs from the current one and has at least **12 characters**. Other sessions are closed. After unlocking the site, you can find the same button under **Settings → Your account**.

If the email service is not active, these operations are unavailable: the application tells you and asks you to try again later.

Access requests, resets and password changes are rate-limited: after **10 attempts** in the same **15-minute** window the operation is suspended for a few minutes.

## How long the session lasts

- The session lasts **7 days** and is remembered by the browser with a secure cookie.
- The **Sign out** button closes the session on the server immediately.
- Closing the browser without signing out does **not** close the session: it stays valid until it expires or you sign out.
- If the session expires or is revoked, the application takes you back to the sign-in form.
